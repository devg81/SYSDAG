-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The project's target population is the GENERAL Indian population,
ages 20-89 (docs/DECISIONS.md D008, D004). `family_history` has been
null for every synthetic patient since M1 because no evidence source
was ever found that covers the full 20-89 range.

The only evidence previously surfaced (docs/M4F_FINAL_CLINICAL_AUDIT.md,
docs/M8_DECISION_BRIEF.md Decision 2) was a single LASI figure covering
ONLY ages 45+. The user correctly objected that generating a field for
less than half the age range, with nothing proposed for the rest, is
not a real plan — it just reproduces the "leave null" outcome for most
patients while adding complexity for the oldest segment only. This
document is the deeper, evidence-backed research the user asked for,
plus a concrete recommended strategy.

2. EVIDENCE GATHERED THIS SESSION (web research, 2026-10-29)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 LASI (Longitudinal Ageing Study in India), Wave 1, 2017-18
    - Sample: 58,734 adults. Geographic scope: all Indian states/UTs
      except Sikkim (near-national).
    - AGE RANGE: 45+ ONLY. This is LASI's whole design — it is a study
      of ageing, it was never fielded on adults under 45.
    - Figures found:
        * Family history of diabetes: 14.33% (already known from prior
          sessions, M4F/M8).
        * Family history of cardiovascular disease (combined "chronic
          heart disease, stroke and CVD", reported by father/mother/
          brother/sister): 11.61% (newly confirmed this session, from
          "Additive interaction of family medical history of
          cardiovascular diseases with hypertension and diabetes on the
          diagnosis of cardiovascular diseases among older adults in
          India", PMC11659756, same LASI Wave 1 cohort).
    - Verdict: REAL, national-scope, nationally-representative evidence
      for TWO conditions (diabetes, CVD) — but structurally limited to
      45+. No amount of further searching changes LASI's own sampling
      frame; this is not a gap that more research can close.

2.2 ICMR-INDIAB (the project's OWN primary source for hypertension/
    diabetes/hyperlipidemia/lipid panel/HbA1c — ages 20+, national,
    113,043 participants, the best-matching population this project
    already relies on elsewhere)
    - CONFIRMED: INDIAB's questionnaire DOES collect family history of
      diabetes and heart disease as a covariate (stated explicitly in
      multiple secondary sources describing its methodology).
    - CONFIRMED: family history of diabetes is used as a RISK-FACTOR
      covariate in INDIAB-derived regression analyses (an odds ratio of
      ~3.13, 95% CI 2.71-3.61, recurs across secondary sources).
    - NOT FOUND: a published POPULATION PREVALENCE PERCENTAGE for family
      history of diabetes or heart disease from the main ICMR-INDIAB-17
      national paper (Anjana et al., Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology,
      2023) or any of its companion papers located this session. The
      paper's own reported headline results are diabetes/prediabetes/
      hypertension/dyslipidemia/obesity prevalence — NOT family history
      prevalence. An odds ratio measures association strength, not what
      fraction of the population has a positive family history — it
      cannot be used as a sampling probability.
    - The full-text Lancet article returned HTTP 403 (paywalled); no
      free PMC/ResearchGate mirror with the relevant table was located.
    - Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED at best. The best-fit population
      (same ages, same national scope as everything else in this
      project) almost certainly has this data in its raw microdata, but
      no accessible publication reports it as a descriptive prevalence
      figure. Not usable without the underlying dataset, which is not
      publicly downloadable and is out of scope to pursue given this
      project's 4-5 week timeline (CLAUDE.md §1).

2.3 WHO STEPS state-level NCD risk-factor surveys (ages 18-69 — the
    best AGE match of anything found, overlapping almost the entire
    target range except 70-89)
    - Punjab (2014-15, n=5,127): family history of diabetes 23.2%,
      family history of hypertension 38.3%.
    - Haryana (2016-18, n=5,078): family history of diabetes 20.8%,
      family history of hypertension 42.6%.
    - Madhya Pradesh and Tamil Nadu STEPS surveys were also located but
      their family-history figures specifically were not extracted this
      session (would need a follow-up fetch of the individual papers).
    - Verdict: REAL, age-range-appropriate evidence — but STATE-level,
      not national. Only a handful of India's 28 states/8 UTs have a
      published STEPS family-history figure. Combining 2-4 individual
      state surveys into one "national" marginal would require inventing
      a population-weighting methodology across states that were NOT
      selected to be nationally representative when combined — this is
      the exact problem docs/DECISIONS.md D010 already identified and
      REJECTED for a different field (urban/rural BMI combination:
      "that would be an engineering assumption presented as if it were
      sourced evidence, which this project does not do silently").
      The same objection applies here with the same force.

2.4 NFHS-5 (National Family Health Survey, 2019-21)
    - Searched specifically for a family-history-of-disease module.
    - NOT FOUND. NFHS-5's clinical/anthropometric/biochemical component
      DIRECTLY MEASURES blood pressure and blood glucose (the source
      this project already uses for BP, D017) — it does not appear to
      ask about family history of disease as a questionnaire item.
    - Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED. (Consistent with NFHS's design as a
      fertility/health/nutrition survey, not a risk-factor survey.)

2.5 Sources NOT yet checked (identified, not pursued, due to time
    budget — listed so a future session does not have to rediscover
    them):
    - Individual state STEPS papers beyond Punjab/Haryana (Tamil Nadu,
      Madhya Pradesh, and others) for their own family-history figures,
      if the goal later becomes "assemble a multi-state proxy with an
      explicit, disclosed combination caveat" rather than a true
      national figure.
    - LASI Wave 2 (fielded 2022-23) — if its age range or questionnaire
      differs from Wave 1, re-check; not verified this session.
    - A possible numbered ICMR-INDIAB companion paper (the series
      includes INDIAB-18 through at least INDIAB-24, covering topics
      like kidney function, metabolic obesity, gestational diabetes) —
      none located this session specifically reports family-history
      prevalence as a headline result, but the full list was not
      exhaustively checked paper-by-paper.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE HONEST CONCLUSION
-------------------------------------------------------------------
No single source found, in this session or in any prior session, gives
a national, 20-89-covering, directly-published family-history
prevalence figure for any condition. This is not a research failure to
fix with more searching — LASI's 45+ design and the state-only scope of
the STEPS surveys are structural facts about what exists, not gaps in
how hard anyone looked. ICMR-INDIAB is the one source that could close
this gap exactly (matching age range AND national scope), but the
number it would need was not published in any paper this session could
access.

This means: a family_history field that is accurate for the WHOLE
20-89 population, backed by a single clean source, does not currently
exist to generate from. Any implementation today will necessarily be
partial-coverage, exactly the situation the user flagged as
insufficient — the question is how to make that partiality honest,
useful, and consistent with how this project has already solved the
identical structural problem elsewhere.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
4. RECOMMENDED STRATEGY (tiered, modeled on the D017 BP precedent)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This project already had this exact shape of problem once before: BP
evidence (NFHS-5) only covers men to 54 and women to 49, but the target
population goes to 89. D017's solution was NOT to leave BP null for
everyone, and NOT to invent evidence for the uncovered ages — it was to
populate what the evidence actually supports, and explicitly FLAG the
extrapolated portion per record (`extrapolated_beyond_source_age_range`)
rather than silently presenting it as equally well-evidenced. Measured
result: 52.7% of the dataset carries that flag — a known, disclosed
limitation, not a hidden one.

The same shape applies cleanly here:

STEP 1 (recommended now): Populate `family_history` for ages 45+ using
the two verified LASI figures:
    - family_history.diabetes: Bernoulli(0.1433), ages 45+ only
    - family_history.cardiovascular_disease: Bernoulli(0.1161), ages 45+
      only (combined heart disease/stroke/CVD, per LASI's own definition
      — do not split into finer sub-conditions LASI itself did not
      separate)
  Each entry carries:
    - source citation (LASI Wave 1, 2017-18, PMC11659756 for CVD; the
      already-cited LASI diabetes paper for diabetes)
    - an explicit `evidence_age_range: "45+"` field on the record (or
      equivalent), so no downstream consumer can mistake 45+ coverage
      for whole-population coverage
    - `family_history: null` for every patient under 45 (continuing
      current behavior for that segment — NOT a new assumption, the
      status quo for 55%+ of the population)

STEP 2 (do not implement without further explicit approval): If broader
age coverage becomes a hard requirement, the only currently-available
path is the WHO STEPS state-level figures (§2.3), combined with an
EXPLICIT, disclosed combination caveat (e.g., "population-weighted
average of the N states with a published STEPS family-history figure,
NOT representative of states without one") — structurally the same
honesty trade D010 already made for a different field, offered here
only as an option, not a recommendation, since it is a real step down
in evidence quality from LASI.

STEP 3 (not pursued, logged for a future session): obtaining ICMR-
INDIAB's underlying microdata (through a formal data-access request to
ICMR, outside this project's current tooling and timeline) would be the
single best fix, since it would give a 20+ national figure matching
every other risk factor this project already uses INDIAB for. Flagged
as a known, real, currently-inaccessible possibility — not a dead end,
just outside what this session's tools and the project's 4-5 week
timeline can pursue.

-------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT THIS STRATEGY DOES NOT DO
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Does NOT invent a figure for ages 20-44. No source was found; none is
  assumed.
- Does NOT silently extrapolate the 45+ rate downward to younger ages
  (that would repeat the exact error D007 already rejected for age
  distribution: assuming a rate measured on one sub-population applies
  uniformly to a different one, without evidence).
- Does NOT combine LASI with the STEPS state surveys into one blended
  national figure — they are different age ranges, different sampling
  frames, and blending them would hide rather than disclose the
  evidence gap.
- Does NOT treat this as resolved. This is a proposal pending explicit
  sign-off before any schema or generation code changes, per the
  project's standing human-checkpoint convention.
